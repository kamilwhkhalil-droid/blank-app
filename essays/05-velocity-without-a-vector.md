# Velocity Without a Vector

*Board Reading No. 5 in a six-part series on how organizations die.*

---

Quibi raised roughly $1.75 billion. It was run by two of the most experienced executives in media. It moved fast, launched a genuinely novel product format, and was dead in about six months.

Every failure post-mortem reached for the usual suspects — the pandemic, the pricing, the phones-only constraint. The board reading is simpler and more uncomfortable:

**Positive Velocity. Declining Market legitimacy.**

Velocity is the rate at which your capacity to create value is changing. Quibi's was, by any internal measure, spectacular — shipping fast, iterating fast, spending fast. Market legitimacy is whether investors, partners, and the market believe you are viable — whether the environment is buying the direction you are moving in.

Quibi had motion the environment was not buying. Enormous Buffer, real speed, a direction no one was traveling. It was solving a problem — short-form premium mobile video that people would pay for — that the environment had not asked it to solve.

**Speed is only a virtue in a direction.**

This reading matters because it is the one that flatters you most. Every internal metric rewards motion: sprints completed, features shipped, campaigns launched. An organization can watch itself accelerate and mistake the acceleration for progress, because acceleration is measurable daily and direction is only measurable against a market that answers slowly.

The same reading, at lower burn, sits on thousands of boards right now: teams with real velocity, working hard in a direction the market has quietly stopped believing in. The internal dashboard is green. The external one — renewal conversations, partner enthusiasm, the tone of investor updates — is cooling. The gap between those two dashboards is the reading.

An honest limit, worth stating: the board cannot tell you the direction. No scoring system can. What it can tell you is when the market has stopped believing you have one — if you are scoring Market legitimacy as its own channel instead of letting your own conviction stand in for it.

So separate the two questions. *Are we moving fast?* is answered internally. *Is anyone buying where we're going?* is answered only by the environment. When the first answer is yes and the second is drifting toward no, more speed is not the fix. More speed is the failure mode.
