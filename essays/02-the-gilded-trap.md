# The Gilded Trap

*Board Reading No. 2 in a six-part series on how organizations die.*

---

In 2007, General Motors had billions in liquidity. One year later it needed the largest industrial bailout in American history. The post-mortems said "the financial crisis." The financial crisis was the trigger. It was not the cause.

The cause was a reading that GM's balance sheet was specifically constructed to hide:

**Strong Buffer. Weak Floor.**

These sound like the same thing. They are not, and the difference is one of the most common ways large organizations die.

**Buffer is what you have** — cash, redundancy, slack, the stock of absorptive capacity. It moves up and down as you spend and rebuild it.

**Floor is where the cliff is** — debt covenants, labor agreements, pension obligations, a dealer network, fixed costs. The threshold below which recovery is impossible. It barely moves, except when you deliberately renegotiate the obligations that create it.

GM had a healthy Buffer and a catastrophic Floor. Labor agreements, pensions, and a dealer network had built a fixed-cost structure that demanded massive volume. When volume fell, GM could not contract. A company that cannot contract can only collapse. Plenty of cash, and contractually unable to shrink: **well-funded and still trapped.**

Here is the mechanism that hides it: averaging. Fold "financial strength" into one number and a strong Buffer and a weak Floor average into a comfortable middle. The single score was built — not intentionally, but structurally — to conceal exactly this combination. No established health index makes the cut. Altman's Z-score doesn't. Neither does any dashboard that reports "months of runway" as its headline resilience number.

So ask the two questions separately:

1. How long can we operate without revenue? (Buffer)
2. How much can we *shrink* without triggering default, breach, or structural collapse? (Floor)

If the first answer is comfortable and the second answer is "not much," you are not resilient. You are gilded. The cash is real, and it is decorating a trap.

The Floor cannot be fixed by saving more money — that only raises the Buffer. It can only be lowered by renegotiating obligations, and the time to renegotiate is while the Buffer is strong, because no counterparty renegotiates generously with a company that visibly needs it.
